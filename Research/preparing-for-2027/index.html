Preparing for 2027

Compliance has stopped asking whether you had controls. It is asking whether you can evidence they were proportionate to the current threat — and prove it in the time you actually have. This briefing cross-reads twelve 2025/26 benchmark reports against Australia’s 2026–27 regulatory spine.

The argument

Three movements, in this order

The adversary clock is now shorter than every statutory clock. Regulators want dated evidence, not policy documents. AI is simultaneously the threat, the target, and the next compliance artefact.

01 · Velocity

29 minutes vs 72 hours vs 30 days

Average eCrime breakout is 29 minutes; the fastest observed is 27 seconds.1 Australia’s ransomware-payment report is due in 72 hours.24 The NDB assessment window is 30 days.20 Every legal timeline assumes you noticed.

02 · Evidence

Boards must show the last twelve months

FIIG27 plus ASIC’s 8 May 2026 letter26 means a board paper showing controls recalibrated against the current threat picture. For most mid-market organisations, the provider’s qualifications become the board’s exhibit.

03 · AI artefact

Shadow AI is a 2027 filing, not IT hygiene

ADM transparency lands 10 December 2026.44 Australian Standards for AI are slated for early 2027.33 APRA’s finding — adoption outpacing controls — is the sentence that belongs on a slide.30

Theme 2

Threat landscape — speed, identity, edge

Verizon DBIR 2025 (22,052 incidents, 12,195 confirmed breaches) found ransomware in 44% of breaches, vulnerability exploitation as initial vector at 20% (+34% YoY), and third-party involvement doubled to 30%. Edge/VPN exploitation grew nearly eight-fold; only 54% of those vulnerabilities were fully remediated (median 32 days).4

CrowdStrike 2026 GTR — evasive adversary

  • Breakout time 29 minutes (65% faster YoY); fastest 27 seconds1
  • 82% of detections malware-free — valid credentials and native tooling1
  • AI-enabled adversary operations +89% YoY; prompt injection at 90+ organisations2
  • 42% of vulnerabilities exploited before public disclosure1
  • Cloud-conscious intrusions +37%; state-nexus cloud +266%; 35% of cloud incidents were valid-account abuse1
  • ClickFix / fake-CAPTCHA lures +563%; 24 new adversaries named (281+ tracked)1

CrowdStrike 2026 Global Threat Report · 24 Feb 2026

Mandiant, ENISA, Microsoft — the nuance

  • Mandiant: global median dwell rose to 14 days — long-dwell espionage and DPRK IT-worker cases (median 122 days) inflating the external-notification tail. Internal detection improved 10 → 9 days.6
  • IAB-to-secondary-group hand-off collapsed to 22 seconds median (from more than 8 hours in 2022).6
  • ENISA: DDoS dominates incident count (77%), ransomware remains most impactful. Threat convergence — hacktivism, crime and state operations blurring. 4,875 incidents, 1 Jul 2024–30 Jun 2025.7
  • Microsoft: 52% of attacks with known motive were financially driven; BEC (21%) surpassed ransomware (16%) among successful attacks.9

Mandiant M-Trends 2026 · ENISA TL 2025 · Microsoft DDR 2025

Implication: at 29-minute breakout and 22-second hand-off, “detect and respond” without pre-emptive identity controls is mathematically late.16 The stack that matches the corpus is identity threat detection, behaviour-based prevention, and automated response.

Theme 3

AI as surface, tradecraft, and defence

The reports that treat “AI risk” as one number are the ones to discount. Wavestone assessed AI security across 17 domains for the first time: 76% have a policy, 62% apply go/no-go validation, 10% have technical prompt-injection defences. AI security maturity: 38%.1516

Surface

Where the org is exposed

  • CrowdStrike: malicious prompts injected at 90+ organisations2
  • Verizon: 15% of employees routinely use GenAI on corporate devices; 72% via personal email4
  • IBM: shadow AI added US$670K to average breach cost; 97% of AI-related breaches lacked AI access controls11
Tradecraft

How attackers use it

  • CrowdStrike: AI-enabled operations +89% YoY2
  • Microsoft: AI-powered phishing 54% click-through (~4× traditional); synthetic identities +195%9
  • IBM: 1 in 6 breaches involved AI-driven attacks, mainly social engineering12
Defence

Where it actually pays

  • IBM: extensive AI/automation saved US$1.9M per breach11
  • EY: AI automation cut MTTD/MTTR 28% on average47
  • CrowdStrike Threat Hunting 2026: AI agent-triggered alerts growing at 2.5× human-triggered — the non-adversarial AI story45

Governance gap. Maps to OWASP GenAI/LLM Top 1039 and MITRE ATLAS.40 Wavestone’s 76% policy vs 10% control split is the most actionable architect datapoint in the corpus.15 APRA’s April 2026 letter found the same pattern in the regulated community: AI adoption is outpacing the controls designed to govern it.30

Theme 4 · Australia

The 2026–27 regulatory spine

This is the calendar a mid-market board can actually use. Privacy Tranche 2 is a government commitment with no Bill and no date — say “proposed.” Dated obligations below are either in force, legislated with delayed commencement, or ASD guidance with a hard milestone.

In force now

ISM-1917 — crypto in procurement

Since March 2024, future cryptographic requirements must be considered in all new procurements. ASD guidance31

Already live · 2025–26

Ransomware reporting, SOCI, CPS 230, smart devices, AML/CTF

Ransomware payment reporting (30 May 2025).24 APRA CPS 230 (1 July 2025).29 Statutory tort for serious privacy invasions (10 June 2025).42 Smart-device security standards (4 March 2026).24 AML/CTF Tranche 2 pulled real estate, lawyers, accountants and others into reporting from 1 July 2026 — Privacy Act applies regardless of the $3m threshold.59 Legislated

10 December 2026

ADM transparency and the Children’s Online Privacy Code

APP 1.7–1.9: privacy policies must disclose automated decision-making that significantly affects individuals.44 The Children’s Online Privacy Code must be developed and registered the same day.34 Commonwealth agencies’ first mandatory AI requirements also fall due. Legislated

End of 2026

Refined PQC transition plan

ASD/ISM milestone: a plan reflecting security goals, risk tolerance, dependencies and data value. End 2028: transition commenced for critical systems and long-lived sensitive data. End 2030: RSA, DH, ECDH, ECDSA retired.3132 ASD milestone

Australian Standards for AI

Announced 15 July 2026. Office of AI in PM&C. Large data-centre operators to underwrite power and minimise water. Training on Australian books, music, art or news without consent barred.33 Announced

Theme 5

Enforcement is no longer a hypothetical

Federal Court

ASIC v FIIG Securities [2026] FCA 92

$2.5m civil penalties plus $500k costs. ~385GB exfiltrated, ~18,000 clients, data later on the dark web. First civil penalty for cyber failures under general AFSL obligations — and the first case where cyber deficiency was the sole cause, not a supporting count. Justice Derrington: compliance would have cost far less than the penalty.2728

ASIC 26-021MR · [2026] FCA 92 judgment. Apply the direction of travel to any board. Do not import AFSL obligations onto non-licensees.

ASIC 26-092MR · 8 May 2026

“The clock is at a minute to midnight.”

Commissioner Simone Constant to all AFS licensees and their directors. Twelve-point action list. Four board-level governance expectations. The letter itself is to be tabled at ultimate board and risk committees. Cyber resilience is a licensing obligation, not an IT issue — and ASIC is not asking for novel tools. It is asking for evidence the fundamentals work.26

ASIC 26-092MR, 8 May 2026. Read with APRA’s 30 April 2026 letter on AI governance gaps.30

Practical path · SMBs

SMB1001 — the certificate mid-market actually fits

ISO 27001 is an ISMS. Essential Eight is free and has no certificate. Insurers, enterprise procurement and government supply chains want something they can verify. SMB1001 sits in that gap: five cumulative tiers, director attestation through Gold, independent audit at Platinum and Diamond. It is a private Dynamic Standards International scheme, not Australian law — and “not mandatory” is no longer the same as “will not be asked for.”48

Director-attested

Level 1

Bronze

Foundational hygiene. Director self-attestation. No external auditor.

Level 2

Silver

MFA on email, unique accounts, password managers, invoice-fraud controls.

Level 3 · 2026

Gold

EDR, IR plan, cyber insurance, and a responsible AI-use policy. Still director-attested.

Independent audit

Level 4

Platinum

Third-party audit, ongoing monitoring, regular vulnerability assessment.

Level 5

Diamond

SIEM/SOC-style analytics and continuous compliance monitoring.

Use with care. Control counts (Silver ~17, Gold ~27 in 2026 reporting) come from consistent third-party/MSP guides, not a direct read of DSI’s primary document.48 Gold’s new AI-use policy is the control most SMBs will not already have a template for. DSI sets the standard; CyberCert issues the certificate — an MSP can implement, not certify.

What to do

Actions a solutions architect can take

Stage 1 · This quarter

Treat identity as the perimeter

  • Phishing-resistant MFA — Microsoft: modern MFA cuts identity-compromise risk >99%; >97% of identity attacks are still password spray/brute force.9
  • Close the shadow-AI gap: SSO-integrated GenAI, DLP on prompts.11
  • Benchmark MTTD against a 29-minute breakout. Hours means automated response is overdue.1
Stage 2 · This budget cycle

Close policy / control gaps

  • Technical LLM controls mapped to OWASP GenAI Top 10 and MITRE ATLAS — not another policy.3940
  • Dated board evidence against the current threat picture; SOCI-aligned where it applies.26
  • Prioritise Recover. Wavestone’s Recover pillar sits at 44% — the structural laggard.16
  • Ask for the PQC plan. ISM-1917 already makes crypto a live procurement obligation.31
Stage 3 · 12–18 months

Platformise, then instrument AI

  • Rationalise tool sprawl (EY: 44 poorly integrated products) toward a platform / XDR model.47
  • Deploy agentic SOC capability where EY projects adoption doubling — after governance exists. Only ~20% of organisations have embedded AI-governance frameworks.47
  • For SMBs, treat SMB1001 Gold as the evidence artefact insurers and procurement will actually accept.48

What would change this advice. If breakout time stabilises or dwell resumes falling, the automation-ROI case weakens.16 If IBM’s global breach cost reverses upward, “AI containment saves money” needs re-examination.12 If ISC2 reintroduces a workforce-gap number, talent strategy shifts back toward hiring over upskilling.19 Check the FY2025–26 ASD report the week before relying on these ASD figures — it typically publishes in October.37

How to read this

Caveats and methodology

Independent ThinkInfoSec briefing prepared for Subnet Cyber Summit 2026.36 Not an official CrowdStrike, Subnet, or Progress publication. The thesis is synthesis; numbered citations map to the source list below.

Sample types are not comparable

IBM/Ponemon interviews 600 breached organisations.11 Verizon is telemetry and casework (22,052 incidents).4 CrowdStrike, Mandiant and Microsoft are vendor telemetry plus IR.169 WEF, PwC, EY and ISC2 are executive surveys.17184719 ENISA is EU OSINT and reporting (4,875 incidents).7 Wavestone is a Europe-weighted consulting maturity interview (200+ organisations).15 Never average 55.3% “maturity” with a 29-minute breakout time.

Collection windows differ

ENISA and Microsoft cover roughly July 2024–June 2025.710 IBM 2025 covers March 2024–February 2025.11 CrowdStrike 2026 and Mandiant 2026 cover calendar 2025.16 DBIR 2025 is roughly November 2023–October 2024.4 Always check the underlying period before comparing “2025” figures.

Geography and currency distort headlines

DBIR skews North America.4 ENISA is EU-only.7 Wavestone is Europe-heavy.15 IBM’s USD Australia figure understates local-currency trajectory.1114 CrowdStrike APJ and ASD are the region-native sources to lead with in an Adelaide room.4637

Vendor incentive and contested figures

CrowdStrike, Microsoft, IBM and Mandiant sell the relevant solutions. Directionally reliable at this data scale; framings such as “AI saves US$1.9M” align with commercial narratives and should be cited as such.11 ISC2’s former 4.8M workforce gap (reused by WEF) was disputed as perceived need rather than vacancies — ISC2’s retreat from it is the story.19

Legal and SMB1001 limits

FIIG and the ASIC letter are financial-services-specific in strict application.2726 Tranche 2 of the Privacy Act is a commitment, not law. SMB1001 control counts and the 200 vs 250 employee ceiling vary slightly across MSP guides; DSI’s own page did not return a usable primary read at time of writing.48
